## Result

The updated `rag v2` and `rat v3` address the two concrete ambiguities in the prior v1 sheet: the rag now visibly has rough, frayed fabric edges, and the rat has a longer body and thin tail with smaller ears, making it less mouse-like at thumbnail size. Neither looks like a different target in this sheet. The earlier v1 objections remain preserved in `batch5-soundsafari-starter-art-independent-20261004.md`; this v2 review is a separate delta, not a replacement of that history.

The 30 thumbnails cover all 26 Starter words plus `duck`, `dock`, `sock`, and `rock` used by the six whole-word minimal pairs. `map` is included here and reads clearly as a world map. `duck/dock/sock/rock` remain individually distinguishable, but the `dock` image has a specific UK-age vocabulary mismatch: it depicts an isolated wooden jetty without water or a boat. Cambridge's UK learner definition of `dock` is where ships stop and goods are loaded or unloaded; its UK `jetty` entry matches the illustrated structure ([dock](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/dock), [jetty](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/jetty)). At 82px this can be named as a bench/platform rather than a dock. Add recognizable port/ship context for the UK dock sense, or select another familiar whole-word pair while preserving the minimal contrast requirement.

Most silhouettes are centered, readable, and visually coherent as polished isolated objects. The flat illustrated world map is a deliberate style exception but remains semantically unmistakable. The fossil-patterned warm-brown `rock` is still recognizable as stone; its surface detail creates a small baked-object ambiguity, but there is no matching snack distractor in this audited set. The sheet does not prove runtime rendering, image loading, screen-reader labels, generated distractor fairness, audio agreement, or child-age recognition.
