## Verdict

**Pass for the narrow 82px semantic-art correction.** At the contact-sheet size, the replacement shows a clear wooden quay/berth beside blue water and a small boat moored directly alongside it. The water-and-boat context makes the intended maritime “dock” meaning much easier to identify and removes the previous isolated-bench/platform reading. The quay edge remains the main form, the boat is fully visible, and the composition is compact enough for the existing card without visible cropping. In the `duck/dock` pair, the duck remains a clear isolated bird while dock now has recognizable water/boat context.

This matches the UK learner sense of a dock as a place where ships stop ([Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/dock)); the picture is also close to Cambridge’s UK description of a jetty as a waterside structure used for getting on and off boats ([Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/jetty)). The earlier image mismatch recorded in the phonics and v2 art reports is resolved for this replacement. “Dock” may still be less familiar to some six-year-olds than everyday nouns; this static review does not establish child naming or vocabulary familiarity.
