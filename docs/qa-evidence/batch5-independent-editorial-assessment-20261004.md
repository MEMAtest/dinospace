## Per-game rationale and remaining work

### Sound Safari

- **Age-6 teaching — 4.0.** The authored route distinguishes first-sound matching, oral blending, and locating a sound in a word. Prompts use short directives, explain that a speech sound is different from a letter name, and make the target word available after success. However, the core child-facing instruction is to listen, and the essential pure-sound control is unusable while its audio path is missing. This rating reflects a coherent authored lesson with a material delivery gap.
- **Meaningful progression — 4.5 for authored design.** The three chapter skills change from hearing/matching, to blending, to sound-position work, and the full sequence completed on desktop in the 5241 run. Mobile Chapters 2–3 were deliberately not guessed or certified. This rating describes the supported progression design; it does not assert complete two-width auditory delivery.
- **Correctness and fair variation — pending.** Source validators and the desktop run support finite, authored questions and answer consistency when the visible clue makes the item solvable. They cannot establish that a learner can distinguish or blend the intended speech sounds when the isolated recordings are absent. Do not rate sound recognition on text clues or spoken whole-word narration.
- **Visible feedback / visual component — 4.0.** Choice screens and correct/wrong feedback are legible and the 5255 tracker repair clears the 390px hint area. The audio-dependent action still fails: the actual `/audio/phonemes/en/t.mp3` request returned 404 and the UI reports that the recording is not packaged. This is a functional gap, not a visual overlap regression.
- **Reliability / navigation / persistence — 4.0.** The guarded local matrix established much of the chapter flow and the 5255 delta exercised the guarded parent-leave flow, but the actual pure-phoneme request returns 404, leaving a core child-facing listening control unavailable. Replay/sibling/reload checks also remain incomplete across both widths.

**Observed weakness → implementation → acceptance.** The 98468 source audit recorded 659 narration phrases, 11 present, separately from 37 pure-phoneme paths, zero present, representing 32 distinct sounds. They are different assets and purposes. Provide an accepted isolated recording for each distinct sound and alias only the approved same-sound paths. Do not use narration clips, letter names, carrier words or whole spoken answers as substitutes. Retain rights, accent, pronunciation, full decode and human phonics review. Then exercise the actual Hear, blend, and hint controls for all three chapters at 1280×800 and 390×844, completing mobile Chapters 2–3 from heard cues; verify no prompt reveals the target before a choice and that replay/Next/confirmed Back cancel prior playback. Until then, recognition correctness and the formal audio dimension remain pending.

### Spelling Studio

- **Age-6 teaching — 4.0.** The method progresses from copying a fully visible short word, to filling one missing grapheme, to assembling a word from tiles. Repeated letters have separate tiles, and settings gate words on taught graphemes. The chapter map screenshot currently tells the child: “Build strictly decodable words with graphemes your grown-up has taught.” “Strictly decodable” and “graphemes” are specialist terms that are not explained in that child-facing sentence.
- **Meaningful progression — 4.5.** The rendered three chapters change the learner's task rather than merely raising word length. The 5241 matrix completed all six-word chapters at both widths. The authored bands are first sounds, more Phase 2 sounds, and longer/digraph sounds.
- **Correctness and fair variation — 4.5.** The current source has 36, 31 and 34 full-taught eligible words by band, uses taught-grapheme eligibility, distinct tile identities for repeated graphemes, shuffled choices, seeded six-word runs and recent-ID avoidance. The full UI run built words with actual tiles and picture/context clues. Audio-dependent hints remain incomplete, but the spelling task's visible tile correctness is evidenced separately from sound recognition.
- **Visible feedback / visual component — 4.5.** The interface keeps a completed word and its grapheme/phoneme sequence visible until Next, gives a usable wrong-build retry and labels its picture clue. The mobile start screen presents the band and 20-word threshold clearly. The 5255 capture confirms the active controls are not covered by the daily tracker.
- **Reliability / navigation / persistence — unscored.** The full chapter matrix and mobile layout delta passed their stated scopes, but exact same-band best-star replay, sibling separation and reload persistence were not independently shown for this game at both widths. This is insufficient evidence for a reliability score, not a confirmed defect. The phoneme hint path is also unserviceable until clips are packaged; that audio limitation is described separately.

**Observed weakness → implementation → acceptance.** Replace the specialist sentence with child-facing copy such as: “Use the sounds you have learned to build each word. Each chapter has six words. Your finished word stays here until you tap Next.” Keep the Grown-ups setting explanation for “grapheme” and “decodable.” Acceptance: read the exact prompt and band labels at 390px and desktop; confirm the instruction uses familiar words without weakening the taught-sound restriction; retain copy → missing sound → independent assembly; verify the answer is not shown in the missing/assembly rounds, including after a wrong attempt. Separately, when the pure phoneme package is available, verify `Hear sounds as a hint` plays the ordered sounds, not the target word, and cancels on Next and confirmed Back.

### Colour Mixing Lab

- **Age-6 teaching — 4.5.** The first band predicts familiar red/yellow/blue combinations, the second teaches white as lighter and black as darker, and the third transfers recipes to named design briefs. Swatches are labelled in text as well as colour; the authored model explicitly says its swatches illustrate a classroom recipe and that real pigments vary.
- **Meaningful progression — 4.5.** Discover Colour, Light and Dark, and Colour Designer each introduce a new reasoning task. The 5241 matrix completed all three six-task chapters at both widths. The 5255 UI delta unlocked design through ordinary play and used a named sunset brief.
- **Correctness and fair variation — 4.5.** The authored one-part RYB classroom recipe table validates result metadata and rejects unsupported mixes. The 5255 rendered Red + Yellow recipe displayed each input once; the prior duplicate “Added Yellow” chip is gone. The same repair delta observed an incorrect recipe retry, one-use clue, correct green result and held Next.
- **Visible feedback / visual component — 4.5.** Named inputs, the design target, result beaker and “Mixed Green” feedback make the action understandable without relying on hue alone. The 5255 mobile screenshot keeps the current recipe and result separate and the tested active-game screen has no floating tracker over its controls.
- **Reliability / navigation / persistence — unscored.** Full chapter and wrong/hint/correct flows are evidenced, but the retained report did not establish per-game replay-star accounting, sibling isolation and reload at both sizes. The 5255 delta also did not repeat those checks. This is an evidence gap, not a confirmed failure.

**Observed weakness → implementation → acceptance.** The duplicate-ingredient and mobile tracker issues observed on 5241 are repaired within the 5255 scope; no additional recipe or layout rewrite is supported by the retained evidence. Complete only the narrow missing confidence checks: in an ordinarily earned profile, replay a Colour Designer band at both widths, show a positive best-star improvement adds exactly the improved credit and equal/lower replay adds none, reload to confirm the saved palette, and switch to a sibling profile to confirm its palette is separate. Keep the user-facing disclaimer that these are classroom swatches, not a claim of real pigment physics.

### Odd One Out

- **Age-6 teaching — 4.5.** Each mission states the property before the child selects an item, then asks the child to choose why it does not fit. The three routes move from familiar groups, to a single visual feature, to simple number rules; explanations use direct definitions such as even numbers pairing with none left over.
- **Meaningful progression — 4.5.** Picture Groups, Look at the Feature, and Number Detectives each change the classification skill. The 5241 run completed all three six-mission chapters at both widths.
- **Correctness and fair variation — 4.5.** Each authored mission generates three items satisfying the named property and exactly one failing it; its because choices have one canonical explanation. Seeded rules and answer positions vary, and the independent matrix exercised item and reason selection. The rendered mobile example showed three animals against a spade under the named animal rule, with labels.
- **Visible feedback / visual component — 4.5.** The 390px capture shows the rule, hear-rule action, four labeled illustrations and hint in view. The two-step item/reason flow makes the “because” task explicit. Wrong item/reason choices retry without advancing; the correct explanation stays visible with Hear why and Next.
- **Reliability / navigation / persistence — unscored.** Full chapter coverage and ordinary parent navigation are supported by the 5241 report; per-game replay, profile-isolation and reload checks are missing. This assessment cannot assign a numeric reliability rating from that evidence.

**Observed weakness → implementation → acceptance.** No authored-data or rendered answer-ambiguity defect was observed in the reviewed sample, so no game-content rewrite is recommended. Close the same bounded reliability evidence gap as the other games: ordinary replay with new rule order, positive-only best-star credit, sibling isolation, and saved chapter state after reload at both widths. Retain the named-property and two-stage answer flow during that check.
