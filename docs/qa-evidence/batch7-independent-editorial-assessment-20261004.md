## Per-game rationale and remaining work

### Memory Match

- **Age-6 teaching — 4.0.** The game gives one clear task, speaks picture names after actual flips, gives gentle mismatch feedback, and displays concrete tips. Later boards use “Group nearby cards in your mind,” which is less actionable than the earlier row-by-row scan instruction and can be read as grouping by position. The child has to infer how to apply that advice to a shuffled board.
- **Meaningful progression — 4.5.** The final rendered progression is 4, 8, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17 and 18 pairs over named, themed boards. Ordinary visible Next controls unlocked the route, replaying a completed/unlocked board worked, and Dinosaur Discovery remained at 13 pairs after the final art update. The suspected Level 5 regression is not reproduced by the exact-source reports.
- **Correctness and fair variation — 4.5.** Ten seeded layouts per authored level are covered by the source tests; every card pair in every board was matched through ordinary visible play. The UI disables matched cards, turns mismatches back, and keeps a run stable. The full run plus final candidate replays confirms Level 5’s original 13-pair count, not a shortened 12-pair board.
- **Visible feedback / visual component — 4.5.** Pair names are announced after flips, mismatches receive a non-shaming prompt, completed boards yield visible sticker progress, and all 87 tokens have illustrated mappings. The final independent reports review the 28 context placements that previously had only rendered coverage. At mobile width cards measured 82×82px, selectors at least 53.5×48px, and the page had no horizontal overflow. Face-down fronts were not exposed in the scoped reload checks.
- **Reliability / navigation / persistence — 4.5.** Full gameplay at both widths, level unlock/replay, reload-resumed selected board, persisted 10/10 sticker count, parent Back, and a separate profile with no Memory completion are evidenced across the retained lineage. The 1410 sound preference delta verifies mute/unmute persistence and player switching at both sizes. No progress loss or cross-profile sharing defect is identified by these checks.

**Observed weakness → implementation → acceptance.** Make the later memory strategy concrete and tied to the board, for example: “Scan one row at a time. When you turn a picture, remember its row and place. If you see it again, look for the place where its partner appeared.” Keep the existing child-facing speech and visible repeat-tip action. At desktop and 390px, check the tip before play and on an unlocked later board; verify it describes a usable action without implying nearby positions are related. No full matrix rerun is needed for this copy delta.

### Solar System

- **Age-6 teaching — 4.0.** The first-mission instruction explains the discovery → challenge route, and the facts/challenges use short prompts. Several terms are not glossed for a six-year-old, including “magnetic field,” “methane,” “orbits,” and “shared centre.” The Pluto/Charon fact is now more accurate, but “shared centre between them” still needs a short child-facing explanation to make the new wording meaningful.
- **Meaningful progression — 4.5.** Each of the nine destinations has six selectable discoveries, a three-discovery badge threshold, a challenge, planet-specific compare content, and an accumulating passport. All nine challenge states and 54 discoveries were completed in the retained matrix; mobile strip cues and Pluto navigation were exercised. Pluto is clearly identified as a dwarf planet.
- **Correctness and fair variation — 4.5.** The full browser matrix completed every planet’s visible challenge using the rendered fact and option content, including recoverable wrong attempts. Seeded answer ordering is implemented. The bounded source repair resolved five specifically reviewed factual/copy issues and the later rendered delta verified those exact cards at both widths. This score covers the authored questions/facts and reviewed corrections; it does not claim new primary-source research beyond those cited repair items.
- **Visible feedback / visual component — 4.5.** The selected planet, current mission, discovery count, “Did you know?” panel, comparison card and challenge feedback are distinct. The mobile screen provides Previous/More strip controls; the 3D orrery can be rotated by drag/tap and has pause, zoom and reset controls. The retained mobile screenshot shows the passport, navigation cues, planet strip and model without clipped controls; the 40b6 delta brings interactive targets to the 48px minimum. No horizontal overflow or trapped navigation was reported.
- **Reliability / navigation / persistence — 4.5.** Passport totals and challenge completion survived reload, 3 discoveries correctly yielded a 1/9 planet badge after the bounded repair, repeated discovery/challenge credit is guarded by saved keys, and Back returned to Explore. Separate visible profiles showed no cross-game progress bleed. Storage failure warning behavior is implemented and covered by source tests; the retained evidence is local-browser evidence only.

**Observed weakness → implementation → acceptance.** Add short, accurate in-place definitions at first use for the technical terms needed by the fact deck rather than removing the science (for example, “A magnetic field is an invisible shield around Earth”; “Methane is a gas in the planet’s atmosphere”; define orbit as a path around another object). For Pluto, explain the shared centre as “Pluto and Charon orbit a point between them.” Keep the fuller terms available as optional detail. Verify each definition against the cited source, render its fact card and spoken line at both widths, and ensure the added words do not push discovery/challenge controls below a usable view. This is a focused copy/content delta, not a repeat of all 54 fact cards and nine challenges.
