## Per-game reasons

### Count the Stars

- **Age-6 teaching — 4.5.** The bands keep one counting behavior in focus: touch each item once; use organized rows/groups; then count an array or combine two groups. A numbered badge records taps without announcing the answer. The latest production sample showed a visible object board, one-use clue, gentle wrong-answer correction, and a held explanation matching the visible total. The one-object prompt “How many planets did you count?” and singular result “There is 1 planet…” were observed on the promoted copy repair.
- **Progression — 4.5.** Star Garden (1–5, scattered), Constellation Workshop (1–10, rows/groups), and Galaxy Survey (1–20, arrays/two groups) change both the number range and strategy. The full local run completed all six rounds per band at desktop and mobile, unlocked only on band completion, then displayed/replayed the constellation collection. The current generator adds harder-range quotas after an older complete matrix exposed weak randomized totals.
- **Correctness and fair variation — 4.5.** The renderer builds one visible target object per answer unit; distractors are unique and shuffled. Run queues are seeded and avoid recent question IDs when the eligible pool permits; the current quota rule ensures each harder band actually practices its extended range. Full local visual counts and the bounded production wrong/clue/correct sequence agreed. No current contradictory arithmetic or ambiguous target was observed.
- **Visible feedback / visual usability — 4.5 provisional.** Marked items, retry guidance, clue, matching worked total, held Next, and the completion-only collection were exercised. The retained mobile run checked the fixed below-fold answer issue and 64px answer controls; no object overlap or horizontal overflow was reported. Native playback exists in retained runs; subjective audio quality is still pending.
- **Reliability / navigation / persistence — 4.5.** The full local matrix exercised unlock, replay, constellation shelf, reload and sibling-profile separation. Production checks confirmed the Maths parent route, confirmed Back/Keep playing, normal reload to the band selector, and no console or static-asset errors in the tested scope.

### Letter Trace

- **Age-6 teaching — 4.5.** “Follow the path” teaches large uppercase strokes; “Big and little” explicitly pairs letter cases; “Trace and read” adds a decodable initial-sound choice using the configured taught graphemes. Visible start numbers, arrows, forgiving trace tolerances, and one-stroke-at-a-time instructions scaffold the motor task. Content pools reject a chapter start unless eight eligible items are available.
- **Progression — 4.5.** The three eight-round chapters add case transfer and then CVC initial-sound transfer rather than simply changing a score threshold. Retained local runs include pointer chapter work, full Challenge 8/8 at both viewports, ordinary unlock/reload, and the keyboard alternative. The separate exported-diagnostic delta shows keyboard completion is assisted and does not award handwriting mastery.
- **Correctness and fair variation — 4.5.** Letter and word queues are shuffled with recent-letter avoidance where the pool allows; Challenge word choices are decodable from the configured taught set. The full Challenge run checked eight visible letter/word pairs at each viewport, wrong-choice feedback, current-round choice reset, and successful matching. The later candidate repaired the visible keyboard cursor’s backtracking and production evidence verified partial progress updates immediately before Space ends a stroke.
- **Visible feedback / visual usability — 4.5 provisional.** Start markers, per-stroke progress, wrong-start guidance, “Show this stroke,” held success, and the Next action were exercised at desktop/mobile sizes. Keyboard and pointer paths both completed; a prior mobile A attempt that did not follow the complete stroke is preserved as tester-attempt evidence, not a product failure. Current production Trace screenshots show no horizontal overflow and successful visible completion. Human listening remains pending.
- **Reliability / navigation / persistence — 4.5.** Ordinary chapter unlock, badge persistence after reload, guarded Keep playing and exact Read & Write return were covered in the retained runs and production deltas. Diagnostic export showed `keyboardAlternative:true`, `masteryEligible:false`, and `handwritingMastery:false` for a keyboard trace. Console and static assets passed in the scoped evidence.

### Cosmic Tic-Tac-Toe

- **Age-6 teaching — 4.5.** The authored sequence names one objective at a time: complete a line, block a line, then create a fork. The fork is explained as “two ways to win on the next turn,” while a board is visually simple and limited to nine cells. Incorrect cells give a retry and a visible clue is available.
- **Progression — 4.5.** Three tactic chapters each contain three boards; board completion unlocks the next tactic and earns its badge. The local 5215 run solved all boards in all three tactics at both widths, replayed Make a Line with a fresh arrangement, and sampled bot free play. The later final package retained the held explanation and native hint replay/cancel behavior.
- **Correctness and fair variation — 4.5.** The implementation has a single winning-line definition, verifies legal moves and terminal states, and generates tactic-specific seeded layouts using board symmetries. The retained run exercised line completion, blocking, forks, a wrong move/retry, a hint-marked target, a bot legal move, terminal wait and reset. The bot’s move choice checks immediate win and threat handling; no impossible move or contradictory result was observed in these checks.
- **Visible feedback / visual usability — 4.5 provisional.** The 3×3 cells measured about 98px at mobile size; controls were at least 48px, hints visibly marked a square, and success explanations remained until Next. Mobile pages were vertically scrollable without horizontal overflow. Native hint playback/replay was observed in the retained audio session. Human listening remains pending.
- **Reliability / navigation / persistence — 4.5.** Board retry, internal Back guard, Keep playing state, confirmed map return, chapter completion, reload persistence, and Amari/Askia profile separation were exercised locally. Production checked wrong/retry, hint/correct, held Next, reload and exact Thinking & Play parent return at both viewports. Scoped console and asset checks were clean.

### Dino Detective

- **Age-6 teaching — 4.0.** The hunt action is concrete and the clue language uses visible locations. Two facts remain visible after a find, and the target facts are generally short. However, some of the scene facts use technical words without an immediate child-level gloss: the swamp fact says “land saturated with water,” and the cave fact says water can “dissolve limestone.” This is a specific mismatch with the roadmap’s short, concrete age-6 language bar; keeping a useful science term is fine if it is explained in ordinary words.
- **Meaningful progression — 4.5.** Twelve named worlds are arranged in Starter, Growing, and Challenge; five finds per world are required to earn that world’s sticker. The bands change clue form from bright footprints to turning trail to faint trail. The full local matrix completed all 12 worlds at both widths and confirmed completion-only rewards, world-specific facts and stickers, and normal map/replay flow.
- **Correctness and fair variation — 4.5.** Each world has a distinct target species and target/world fact. Every round has one correct cover among five safe positions; the five-place arrangement is permuted and recent signatures are avoided. The full local run checked targets, wrong covers, clue-directed covers, repeated taps, all 12 rewards, and a different-seed replay. The bounded production run confirmed the clue target corresponded to the found creature and both fact cards.
- **Visible feedback / visual usability — 4.5 provisional.** Five numbered covers offer a clear touch-sized choice; retained measurements found at least 56px on mobile. “Not this cover,” clue location, revealed species, two held facts and Next find were visible. The production run found no console/static asset errors in the tested route; packaged same-origin narration advanced and paused/canceled on mute or confirmed exit. Human listening remains pending.
- **Reliability / navigation / persistence — 4.5.** The full local run completed all 12 worlds at both widths, reloaded saved map/sticker state, and used the built-in sibling profile switcher. Confirmed Back and Keep playing were exercised; production confirmed Explore & Languages parent routing and that a partial search did not become a completion on reload. Diagnostics were exported via the visible grown-up control and contained bounded event metadata rather than child names or answer text.

## Action to close the only observed editorial weakness

For Dino Detective, simplify the swamp and cave scene facts while keeping their science meaning:

| Current fact | Suggested child-facing wording |
|---|---|
| “A wetland is land saturated with water, though it may not stay wet all year.” | “A wetland is a place where the ground stays very wet. Some wetlands dry out for part of the year.” |
| “Water can slowly dissolve limestone and help form caves.” | “Water can slowly dissolve (wear away) limestone rock and help caves form.” |

Acceptance check: use the ordinary reveal flow for Swamp and Cave at desktop and 390px; confirm both revised facts stay visible with the target fact until Next, preserve the science relationship, and remain short enough to read. Verify each exact updated phrase has a matching authored mapping and packaged clip, then bind the changed source and served assets. A human reviewer must still listen to the chosen clips; no copy or decode check can close that requirement. The `f609cc1` source edit and mapping test are not credited in the scores above.

No additional implementation change is recommended for the other three games from the evidence reviewed here. Their scored dimensions are supported by current authored content and retained gameplay evidence; their final award remains blocked only by the separately open human-listening gate, not by an unobserved defect inferred from missing reruns.
