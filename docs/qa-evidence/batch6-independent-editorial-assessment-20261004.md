## Per-game rationale and remaining work

### Pattern Parade

- **Age-6 teaching — 4.0.** The visible unit clue and held explanation are concrete and, in the new mobile ABB case, correctly match all three first terms. However, the normal question label says `ABB pattern 3: what comes next?` before the child responds. The letters reveal the exact pattern type and weaken the child’s chance to infer the repeating unit. The visible first-three clue then reinforces it. The small decorative hero image is not itself a usability defect; the sequence tokens are the visual focus and remain legible.
- **Meaningful progression — 4.5.** Repeat it, Change the rule and Growing festival move through AB/AAB/ABB/ABC units and growing patterns with picture, sound and movement variants. The full matrices completed all three chapters at both widths. The full-content signature queue avoids duplicate pattern variants within the tested run/replay scope.
- **Correctness and fair variation — 4.5.** Source validators enforce one answer, valid AB/AAB/ABB/ABC structures and supported growth rules; 54 authored variants per band support distinct runs. The prior AAB clue failure remains preserved for 5259, and later rendered candidates show the repaired three-term clue. The 5295 mobile ABB scenario matches the visible sequence, answer and held explanation.
- **Visible feedback / visual component — 4.5.** The mobile sequence has distinct symbol cards, answer choices, a one-use clue, held “Why it works” fact and explicit Next. The 390px screenshot leaves the full pattern and options visible. The tiny portrait header art is decorative and nonblocking, so no visual deduction is made for it.
- **Reliability / navigation / persistence — unscored.** Full chapter completion, desktop replay and the mobile tracker repair are evidenced; a per-game ordinary reload and sibling-progress isolation check is not established by these retained reports.

**Observed weakness → implementation → acceptance.** Replace the pre-answer label with neutral copy such as “What comes next?” and reserve “ABB pattern” for the held result/fact after selection. Keep the visible sequence and clue. Acceptance: at both widths sample AB, AAB and ABB through ordinary runs; confirm no rule code/name appears before a child answers, the clue is consistent with the visible unit, the answer is unique, and the rule explanation stays until Next. Reuse retained full matrices; this is a copy delta, not a new 18-question run.

### Dino Hangman

- **Age-6 teaching — 4.0.** The routes build from word families to picture clues to independent rescue. The actual 390px screen gives a simple definition, the family suffix, separate letter buttons and a visible rescue-supply count; the held answer explains the built word and keeps the fact visible. `Word family` and the shorthand `-in` are instructional terms that are not explained in the child's first chapter screen.
- **Meaningful progression — 4.5.** Word-family rescue, picture-clue rescue and independent rescue change the support available across three purposeful routes, with six questions each. The retained full matrix completed all 18 at both widths, including zero-supply failure and same-word retry without losing the prompt.
- **Correctness and fair variation — 4.5.** The authored pool uses explicit grapheme segmentation and filters by taught sounds with no untaught fallback; current source has stable word IDs, frozen question order and randomized letter options. The visible mobile `TIN` prompt (“A metal container for food”) matches its `-in` family, and the repair delta confirms clue labels use letter names (“find F/M”) rather than pretending they are pure sounds. The zero-supply retry and held word fact were exercised in the full run.
- **Visible feedback / visual component — 4.5.** The retained mobile screen uses a friendly dinosaur, picture clue, family ending, blanks, rescue-supply counter and clear letter grid; a completed word/fact remains visible with an explicit Next. The daily tracker no longer covers the centered action in the 5267 repair delta.
- **Reliability / navigation / persistence — unscored.** The full route and retry flow are demonstrated, but saved word/reward reload and sibling isolation are not established for this game by the retained evidence.

**Observed weakness → implementation → acceptance.** Replace `Word family: -in` with child-facing wording such as “Words ending in -in” and explain that the blank word shares that ending. Keep the later picture clue and independent rescue less revealing. Acceptance: show the exact copy at 390px and desktop; confirm the family clue identifies the shared ending without disclosing the missing letters; exercise a wrong letter, zero-supply recovery and correct held fact. Existing matrices cover gameplay; this is a bounded copy clarification.

### Chess Explorers

- **Age-6 teaching — 4.0.** Missions give one objective and the coordinate-specific repair improved clue wording. The rendered queen question nevertheless places the full move rules for rooks, bishops, queens, knights, kings, pawns, blockers, check, castling and promotion in a single paragraph above the board. That is more rule information than the current one-piece mission needs and competes with the objective.
- **Meaningful progression — 4.5.** Piece Moves, Safe Captures and Mini Puzzles change the task from legal movement, to safe enemy capture, to board objectives. The retained matrices completed five puzzles per chapter at both widths, with a targeted mobile/desktop delta for coordinate-specific king/knight clues and legal-but-wrong retry.
- **Correctness and fair variation — 4.5.** Canonical puzzle validation covers legal movement, bounds, blockers, friendly occupancy and defended captures. The independent run showed a blocked knight square was not offered as a legal destination, a wrong legal square remained retryable, a successful move cleared stale error feedback, and a safe capture visibly removed the piece. The source accurately states that check, castling and promotion are outside this mini-board.
- **Visible feedback / visual component — 4.5.** The screenshot's gold-star goal, blue selected piece and green legal squares distinguish objective, selection and allowed moves. The board is centered and large enough to understand at desktop; mobile all-chapter completion is retained. No visual collision or clipping was reported.
- **Reliability / navigation / persistence — unscored.** Completion and Next are evidenced in the full matrix, but per-game reload and sibling-isolation checks are absent from the retained evidence.

**Observed weakness → implementation → acceptance.** Replace the entire always-visible rule paragraph with one mission-specific instruction (for example, “A queen moves straight or diagonally. Move it to the star.”); place other-piece rules in a short optional “How pieces move” panel. Acceptance: check a rook, knight, pawn capture and safe-capture mission at both widths; show only the relevant rule before a move, preserve the mini-board limitation notice where needed, and keep the legal squares/goal visible without pushing the board or action below usable view.

### Astronaut Academy

- **Age-6 teaching — 4.0.** Science, engineering and review are clear routes, and the official-source facts use concrete examples. The implemented Mission clue currently displays the mission's post-answer fact before the child chooses. For the rendered atmosphere question, “Earth’s atmosphere is a layer of gases around our planet” directly names the requested answer to “What is the blanket of gases around Earth called?” This violates the roadmap's pre-answer clue boundary.
- **Meaningful progression — 4.5.** Space science introduces Sun/Moon/Earth/Mars facts; Mission engineering applies tools and spacecraft; Review Missions revisit missed ideas on later runs rather than replacing the active question. The full three-chapter matrix completed at both widths, and the mobile passport retained six distinct chapter facts and 18 across the three chapters.
- **Correctness and fair variation — 4.0.** Authored missions validate answer uniqueness and official NASA/JPL/ESA source links; the full UI observed relevant clues, a wrong solar-panel selection followed by correction, and a held source card. However, the current pre-answer clue repeats the answer-bearing fact, which makes a correct choice possible without independent reasoning. Source validity does not cure that interaction defect.
- **Visible feedback / visual component — 4.0.** The mission prompt, icon, answer choices and passport are clear, and the fact/source card holds after success. Before-answer clue content is the defect: it prints the full answer-bearing fact in the question card, so the child's choice is revealed rather than scaffolded.
- **Reliability / navigation / persistence — unscored.** Mission completion, passport accumulation, chapter progression and immediate replay variation are evidenced. The reports do not establish ordinary reload persistence and sibling isolation for this game.

**Observed weakness → implementation → acceptance.** Give each mission a separate authored child-facing clue field instead of displaying `mission.fact` as the hint. Keep the factual statement and source on the successful held card/passport. A clue may direct attention to a relevant idea but must not repeat the correct option or its full definition. Acceptance: review the visible and spoken clue for every authored mission against its question/options; test representative Sun/Moon, rover/rocket and atmosphere questions at both widths; confirm the clue narrows attention without stating the answer, wrong choices remain retryable, and the full source-backed fact appears only after a correct answer and stays until Next.
